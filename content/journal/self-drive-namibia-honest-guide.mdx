Namibia is made for driving: empty roads, enormous views, and the freedom to stop wherever the light is good. Thousands of visitors self-drive every year, and most of them have the trip of their lives.

But it isn't for everyone, and we'd rather tell you that now than after you've booked.

## Most of the roads are gravel

Namibia has more gravel road than tarmac. The main gravel roads are usually wide and well graded. They are not hard to drive, but they are different.

- **Speed is the danger.** On gravel, a car that feels steady at 100 km/h can slide at the first loose corner. We recommend around 80 km/h, slower on corners and crests.
- **Corrugations** (ripples across the road) shake everything. They're tiring, not dangerous.
- **Sand and loose patches** appear without warning. Slow down early.

Every journey on our site shows how much of each day is gravel and how long you'll be at the wheel. We think you should know before you go.

## Distances are real

Towns can be 200 km apart. There may be no phone signal for hours. Fill up with fuel whenever you can, even with half a tank left, and carry water.

## Don't drive at night

Animals cross the roads, and there are no lights and little traffic. We plan every day to get you to your lodge by late afternoon, which also happens to be when the light is best.

## The car matters

For most of our routes we recommend a 4x4 with high clearance and two spare tyres. You might not need four-wheel drive often, but you'll be glad of the clearance and the stronger tyres. If you're camping, a rooftop tent, fridge and camp kitchen come with the car.

## Who self-drive suits

- Confident drivers who enjoy long days on the road
- People who like to stop on a whim
- Families who want their own space and pace
- Anyone travelling for two weeks or more on a sensible budget

## Who it doesn't

- First-time drivers on the left, or on gravel, who'd rather not learn on holiday
- Anyone who'd be anxious about a puncture in the heat
- Travellers with less than about ten days, where long drives eat the trip

If that's you, a guide or a few short flights can give you the same places without the driving. Our [chooser](/how-to-travel#chooser) will give you an honest suggestion in five questions.

## What we give every self-driver

A route timed for the light, a printed road book, offline maps, a briefing on arrival, a vehicle with two spare tyres, and a phone number answered day and night.
