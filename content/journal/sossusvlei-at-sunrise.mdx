The dunes of Sossusvlei are at their best for a very short time. At first light the ridges catch the sun while the valleys stay in deep shadow, and the sand goes from grey to apricot to red in minutes. By mid-morning the light flattens, the heat builds and the magic is gone.

So the whole day turns on one question: where will you be at sunrise?

## The gate

The park gate at Sesriem opens at sunrise. If you stay outside the park, that's the earliest you can enter, and you'll join a queue of cars waiting to go in.

Guests staying at a lodge or campsite inside the gate can usually leave earlier, which means being on the dunes as the sun comes up rather than driving towards them. If sunrise matters to you, this is the single most important booking decision of the trip. **Check current gate times when you book: they change through the year.**

## The drive in

From Sesriem it's about 60 km of tarmac to the 2x4 parking area, and then 5 km of deep sand to Sossusvlei and Deadvlei. You can take the park shuttle for the sand stretch or, if you're confident, drive it in a 4x4 with lowered tyre pressure. Allow an hour from the gate to Deadvlei.

On the way you'll pass Dune 45. It's popular for sunrise because it's close to the road. It's beautiful, and it's busy.

## Our favourite plan

1. **Leave in the dark** if you're staying inside the gate. Bring coffee.
2. **Go straight to Deadvlei.** Walk in while the pan is still in shadow. The trees against the lit dunes behind them are the picture everyone wants, and it only works early.
3. **Climb a little.** You don't need to climb Big Daddy (the tallest dune here, and a hard slog). Even 50 metres up a ridge gives you the curve of the crests.
4. **Leave by 10am.** It gets hot quickly. Rest in the shade at midday, and come back for Sesriem Canyon or Elim Dune in the afternoon.

## Sunset

Sunset light is lovely too, but the park gate closes at sunset. That makes the far dunes hard to reach in the evening unless you're staying inside. Elim Dune, a few kilometres from the gate, is our favourite evening walk: close enough to watch the last light and still be out in time.

## When to go

Sossusvlei is good all year. From April to September the days are cooler and the nights cold. December to February can pass 40 °C by midday, so plan to be finished early.

> The light won't wait. But if you're in the right place, you won't need it to.
